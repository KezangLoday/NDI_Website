Dechen finishes her degree. Everything that comes next will ask her to prove who she is.

Job forms, bank forms, campus forms — the same documents photocopied again and again.

Offices across Thimphu, queues and stamps, and a trip back for the one paper she forgot.

Weeks lost to verification. Her identity sits in filing cabinets she cannot reach.

She downloads the wallet. Her foundational ID is issued to her phone, held only by her.

Degree, census, licence — signed by the issuer, tamper-proof, and stored with her.

A scan and a tap. She shares only the fields a service asks for — and nothing else.
